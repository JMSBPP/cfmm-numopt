<objective>
REPR-02 + REPR-04: one η, one bridge, and co-compilation.

Today `TradingRegion.gms` declares `Set inventory / assetX, cashY /` and
`PricingKernel.gms` declares `Set inventory / X, Y /`. They cannot be compiled together
without `$onMulti*`, which is lint-forbidden because it **silently replaces** a symbol
at rc=0.

And η exists in three encodings with no bridge: `eta_x_y = precision` read as
`eta_x_y/unity` (a WAD ratio), `etaQ128 = 2^127` (Q0.128), and the dimensionless
argument `tunablePricingKernel(di, i, eta)` takes. The WAD-as-Q0.128 collapse is the most
dangerous conflict in the repo **because it fails silently in both directions**: reading a
WAD-scaled η through the Q0.128 divisor gives ≈1.5e-21, the exponent collapses, and the
kernel becomes a flat, tick-independent `λ^0 = 1` — which passes **every positivity
assertion in this repository**.

Purpose: a flat kernel is indistinguishable from a correct one under the assertions that
exist today. This plan makes it distinguishable.
Output: `etaCanon` + two bridge macros, a tick-sensitivity assertion, the reconciled
`inventory` set, a co-compilation unit, and three committed mutants.
</objective>

<binding_facts>
0. **"Bridge" is overloaded in this plan — read this first.** REPR-02's requirement text
   says η's second representation "carries a documented **bridge**", meaning a **scale
   conversion** (`etaFromQ128`, `etaFromWad`). VOL-00 reserves **BRIDGE** as an *epistemic
   tier*: a GAMS-established link with no Lean counterpart. They are unrelated ideas that
   share a word, and this plan uses the first sense roughly eight times. Where the tier is
   meant, it is written in capitals as `TIER: BRIDGE`. Do not let a reader conclude that a
   scale conversion is an epistemic claim, or vice versa; say "conversion" in new prose
   where the requirement text does not force the older word.
1. **`$onMulti*` is lint-forbidden (LINT-04)** — it silently *replaces* a symbol at rc=0.
   REPR-04's co-compilation must be achieved by having ONE declaration, not by
   suppressing the collision.
2. **Duplicate declarations — MEASURED, and the `$150` story this plan first carried was
   wrong three ways.** Probed on GAMS 54.1:
   - two `Set inventory` declarations with data → **rc=2**,
     `**** 194 Symbol redefined - a second data statement for the same symbol`. The
     diagnostic is **`$194`**, not `$150`.
   - two `Scalar` declarations of the same identifier → **rc=0. Tolerated. Silent.**
   - `$onMulti`/`$onMultiR` **do** suppress the `Set` case — which is precisely why
     LINT-04 forbids them, and why "unconditional" was wrong.

   The rc=2 expectation for `inventory_redeclared.gms` **survives**; only the mechanism
   and the diagnostic number change. The wider lesson belongs in the SUMMARY: the
   collision is real for `Set`s and **invisible for `Scalar`s**, so REPR-01's sole-definer
   rule is enforced by LINT-09 and by deletion, never by the compiler.
3. **`etaQ128 = power(2,127)` is the Q0.128 encoding of exactly 0.5**, and
   `2^127 / 2^128 = 0.5` is exact in binary. Therefore replacing the hard-coded
   exponent divisor `2` in `sqrtPX96_at` with a multiplication by `etaCanon = 0.5`
   is **bit-identical**: `x*0.5` and `x/2` are the same IEEE operation (an exact scaling
   by a power of two). Both committed GDX fixtures must therefore regenerate
   content-identically — that is a *verification*, not an aspiration.
4. **The measured defect this closes:** `sqrtPX96_at` hard-codes its exponent divisor as
   the literal `2`, so the model **structurally cannot vary η**, while `etaQ128` is
   exported as provenance and read by nobody. Plan 01-05 registers the mutation proof
   that this change makes possible.
